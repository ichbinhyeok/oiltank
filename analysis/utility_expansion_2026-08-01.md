# Oil-tank utility expansion — 2026-08-01

Google Ads Keyword Planner, United States, July 2025–June 2026.

## Discovery

A second expansion tested 50 homeowner utility and ownership queries that were missing from the removal-led universe.

- 6 keywords: 1,000–10,000 monthly.
- 24 keywords: 100–1,000 monthly.
- 17 keywords: 10–100 monthly.
- 3 unmeasurable/not found.

Raw utility-only scenarios before semantic overlap:

| Scenario | Searches/month |
|---|---:|
| Low | 8,570 |
| Geometric-midpoint base | about 27,100 |
| High | 85,700 |

The six head terms are `heating oil tank`, `275 gallon oil tank`, `275 gallon heating oil tank`, `oil tank sizes`, `oil tank dimensions`, and `oil tank gauge`.

## SERP observation

The utility SERPs are materially weaker than the national removal-cost and replacement-cost SERPs. Results include small niche HVAC sites, local mechanical contractors, old PDF charts, product specification documents, Inspectapedia, and a small number of calculators. They are not uniformly controlled by Angi, HomeAdvisor, Forbes, or other giant lead marketplaces.

This suggests one defensible traffic product rather than many thin articles:

## Residential Oil Tank Identifier & Capacity Tool

Inputs:

- tank orientation and shape;
- length, width/diameter, and height;
- gauge fraction or measured stick depth;
- nominal tank label if known;
- household fuel usage;
- installation location and approximate age;
- rust, oil odor, moisture, or visible leak symptoms.

Outputs:

- likely tank size and dimensions;
- estimated gallons remaining;
- estimated usable fill capacity;
- days of heat remaining;
- matching gauge chart;
- replacement-age and leak-risk prompts;
- a separate removal, replacement, repair, or fuel-delivery route.

One strong interactive surface can target the size/dimensions/275-gallon/chart/capacity family without producing doorway pages. A separate gauge troubleshooting page/tool can target the gauge family. Repair, installation, and leak pages should remain distinct commercial routes.

## Impact on the 100-click/day question

Adding the raw utility base (about 27.1k) to the earlier commercial base (23.1k) produces about 50.2k searches/month before within-cluster and cross-cluster overlap. The combined raw high ceiling is about 158.7k. Therefore the utility discovery changes the verdict from a near-hard ceiling to a plausible high-case traffic path.

It does not make 100 clicks/day the base case. Size, dimension, 275-gallon, chart, and gauge variants overlap heavily; informational utility traffic also converts to removal/replacement leads at a lower rate than direct service searches. The business needs separate GSC proof for traffic and platform proof for accepted-call conversion.
